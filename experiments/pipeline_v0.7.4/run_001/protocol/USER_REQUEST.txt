# 2. PRIMARY RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

The research has established that LLMs can:

1. Generate stochastic microstructures from real microscopy references.
2. Produce structurally similar particle and fiber composites.
3. Generate structures that can be analyzed mechanically.
4. Revise microstructure generators to alter mechanical stiffness.

However, two limitations remain.

First, the previous stiffness-maximization experiment created artificial segmented reinforcement lanes instead of preserving irregular reference-like particles.

Second, measured mechanical feedback did not outperform an objective-only control in the small initial study.

The next scientific questions are:

**Question A:** Can an LLM generate P3-like microstructures that match a prescribed apparent modulus while preserving exact composition and the stronger morphology constraints?

**Question B:** Does informative geometry–mechanics feedback improve target-property matching compared with a matched revision that does not receive measured mechanical results?

**Question C:** Can the established mechanical workflow be applied to a genuinely different three-phase microstructure family, F1?

The STL deliverable serves the additional goal of communicating the research physically to an audience.

It must not be confused with evidence of mechanical performance or physical material calibration.
