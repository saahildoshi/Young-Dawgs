# 9. THE NEW MECHANICAL OBJECTIVE

Do not continue maximizing vertical stiffness.

The new primary objective is:

**Match a prescribed apparent vertical elastic modulus while preserving exact composition and reference-like morphology.**

Define:

`J = E_y_app / E_matrix`

where:

- `E_y_app` is the apparent vertical modulus under the registered P3 finite-domain mechanical protocol.
- `E_matrix = 1000 MPa`.

Let the prospectively registered target be:

`J_target`

Equivalently:

`E_target = J_target * E_matrix`

For an accepted realization:

`target_error = abs(J - J_target) / J_target`

Lower error is better.

Also report signed error:

`signed_target_error = (J - J_target) / J_target`

A positive signed error means the specimen is too stiff.

A negative signed error means it is too compliant.

Do not reward excessive stiffness merely because it exceeds the target.

The purpose is target-property inverse design, not unconstrained maximization.

# 10. TARGET ACCURACY AT GENERATOR LEVEL

The generator produces a stochastic family, not one deterministic specimen.

Therefore, distinguish:

## A. Mean-property matching

`mean_bias = abs(mean(J) - J_target) / J_target`

This measures whether the generator's expected apparent modulus is near the target.

## B. Typical realization accuracy

`mean_absolute_target_error = mean(abs(J_i - J_target) / J_target)`

This measures how closely individual generated realizations approach the target.

## C. Mechanical variability

Report:

- Sample standard deviation.
- Coefficient of variation.
- Minimum and maximum.
- Median target error.
- Target-hit rate.

A generator could obtain the correct mean while producing individual specimens far above and below the target.

Do not classify that as equivalent to consistently generating near-target specimens.

Use mean absolute target error across held-out realizations as the primary numerical performance metric for comparing revision scripts.

Report mean-property bias and variability as additional outcomes.

Register this metric hierarchy before collecting LLM responses.

# 16. NEW MORPHOLOGY DESCRIPTORS

Implement the smallest defensible set of additional descriptors needed to distinguish irregular dispersed particles from highly organized reinforcement lanes.

Reuse existing validated categorical descriptors wherever appropriate.

Add the following principal descriptor families.

## A. Area-weighted shape statistics

For each measurable connected reinforcement region:

- Region area.
- Major-axis length.
- Minor-axis length.
- Aspect ratio.
- Principal orientation.

Report:

- Existing unweighted mean aspect ratio.
- Area-weighted mean aspect ratio.
- Area-weighted upper-tail aspect ratio.
- Area fraction contained in highly elongated regions.
- Largest-region shape statistics.

The area-weighted quantities are essential.

A small number of large elongated regions should not disappear statistically because many small regions have low aspect ratios.

## B. Orientation organization

Measure axial orientation using the correct 180-degree equivalence.

Potential descriptors:

- Area-weighted orientation tensor.
- Axial alignment order parameter.
- Preferred orientation angle.
- Area fraction associated with strongly vertical orientations.
- Vertical orientation tensor component.

Handle nearly circular regions carefully.

Their principal-axis orientation may be numerically unstable and should not dominate the orientation statistic.

Define an appropriate shape or anisotropy weighting.

## C. Directional spatial correlations

Measure reinforcement two-point correlation along horizontal and vertical directions at several registered distances.

Include short, intermediate, and long ranges.

Use finite-domain normalization.

Do not wrap the image periodically.

A useful diagnostic is the difference between vertical and horizontal correlation:

`S2_y(r) - S2_x(r)`

Use a registered aggregate of these differences.

## D. Reinforcement occupancy organization

Measure:

- Reinforcement occupancy of each image column.
- Reinforcement occupancy of each image row.
- Variation in column and row occupancy.
- Occupancy variation in wider strips.
- Long-range persistence of reinforcement-rich vertical strips.

Do not rely on a single strip width.

A segmented lane pattern may evade a descriptor that examines only individual columns.

## E. Gap-tolerant connectivity

Investigate descriptors that detect nearly connected reinforcement paths even when the reinforcement remains formally disconnected.

Examples:

- Largest-component growth under small geometric dilation.
- Near-spanning reinforcement paths after a one-pixel or two-pixel gap closure.
- Directional continuity under a registered limited-gap criterion.

These are diagnostic transformations used by the evaluator.

Do not modify the actual mechanical specimen.

Do not automatically interpret dilated connectivity as a genuine physical reinforcement connection.

The purpose is to detect the nearly continuous spatial organization that the old connectivity policy missed.

## F. Spatial dispersion and clustering

Reuse or extend:

- Nearest-neighbor spacing.
- Spacing coefficient of variation.
- Centroid spatial distribution.
- Pair-distance statistics.
- Local area-fraction variation.
- Interface density.

Do not add redundant descriptors without a clear scientific purpose.

# 35. PROSPECTIVE TARGET-SELECTION RULE
